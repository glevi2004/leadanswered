# Lu Computer — Manifesto

## The thesis

Every business runs on software it resents. A dozen dashboards — a CRM, an invoicing app, a scheduler,
a website builder, a help desk — each a tool you have to log into and *operate*. The software never does
the work. It just gives you somewhere to do it yourself. That isn't a business problem; it's how all
software works. You are always the operator.

Agents change what a computer can be. They write software, operate machines, hold conversations, send
mail, make calls — they *do the work*, not just give you a place to do it. So the computer itself should
be agent-native: **you command, it does.**

**Lu Computer is that computer.** Not another dashboard, not another editor — an **operating system where
a fleet of agents does real work on real infrastructure**, and you watch, steer, and approve.

## What it is — a real computer

Not a metaphor. Lu Computer has the faculties of an actual computer:

- **A screen** — the canvas, where you watch the work happen.
- **A shell** — the cloud terminal, a real machine you can open and type into.
- **A phone** — a real number. Text or call Lu; Lu texts you back. Your leads text it; it answers.
- **An inbox** — a real email address. The computer sends and receives mail — it emails your customers;
  you email it a task.
- **A filesystem** — the Library: files, artifacts, and the **Context** that remembers how you work.
- **Programs** — the agents, each a worker with its own machine and tools.
- **Distros** — the **presets**: boot it as a business, a dev studio, a personal assistant, or wire your own.
- **Permissions** — approvals: it asks before it spends, ships, or sends.

Its **I/O is real in both directions**: you drive it by *text, email, or the canvas*; it acts on the world
by *email, SMS, code, and deploys*. That is what makes "it's an OS" concrete instead of a slogan.

## One computer, many shapes

The **kernel** is general — a canvas, **Lu** the orchestrator, and agents that each get a real machine.
On top of it, **presets** boot the computer for a use, each arriving with a Lu-generated **roadmap** its
agents work down:

- **Business** *(the front door)* — department agents run your company.
- **Studio / Dev** — a fleet of coding agents build and ship software. *It's how we build Lu with Lu.*
- **Personal** — your own agents for your own work.
- **→ Custom** — compose your own agents and wire your own I/O.

You start from a preset and reshape it however you like — a **dev tool**, an **automation tool**, a
**business OS**, or whatever you point it at. Presets become a shareable library over time.

**The north star:** *Cursor put one agent in your editor. Lu puts a company of them on your computer.*

## Why now

- **Agents can do real work.** They write software, hold conversations, operate a computer, send mail and
  messages — the frontier crossed from "assist" to "do."
- **People want the outcome, not the tool.** "A website," not a website builder. "Paid," not an invoicing
  app. "Handled," not a help desk.
- **Work is a set of jobs to be done.** Give each job an agent, put them on one computer under one
  orchestrator, with real peripherals to act through, and the software *is* the team.

## The principles

1. **Agents do, dashboards don't.** The unit of value is a job finished, not a screen to operate.
2. **A real computer, real peripherals.** Screen, shell, phone, inbox — real I/O, real infrastructure. The
   Engineer builds in a real sandbox and ships to a real URL. No mockups, no theater.
3. **One computer, one canvas.** Lu at the center, agents around it. You watch the work happen — you don't
   hunt across twelve tabs.
4. **You stay in command.** Agents propose; you approve what matters — spending, shipping, sending. Staged,
   legible, never a surprise.
5. **Start from a preset, shape it however you like.** Boot it as a business, a studio, or your own thing;
   the longer it runs, the more its Context knows how *you* work.

## Who it's for

Anyone with work to be done and no wish to become a software operator. We **lead with business** — the
Business preset, where an owner points Lu at their operations and the back office simply *runs* — because
that's where the leverage and the willingness to pay are clearest. We **dogfood with Dev** (we build Lu
with Lu), and open the computer to personal and custom uses as it matures.

## The bet

The company of the near future is not a team operating software. It is a founder and a computer full of
agents that do the work — one you text, email, and watch. **Lu Computer is that computer.**
