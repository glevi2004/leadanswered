## 5. Claude's opinionated take (asked for, so: no hedging)

1. **You're right to dogfood first — but timebox it or it becomes hiding.** "Use it myself
   before pitching anyone" is correct for exactly ONE reason: you can't sell a loop you've
   never run. But the product will never FEEL ready — that feeling isn't the gate. Put a date
   on it: run Lead Answered on Lead Answered for two weeks, then the first outside design
   partner gets onboarded THAT week regardless of how the product feels. A date converts
   dogfooding from procrastination-with-good-conscience into a sprint with a demo at the end.
2. **You don't need the whole app to earn the first customer.** The wedge — Sarah answers the
   line, books estimates, chases quiet leads, runs the review wave — is LIVE or one week out.
   Quotes/Invoices/Analytics are not what makes a roofer say yes; the answered phone is.
   Don't gate outreach on modules the customer won't touch in month one.
3. **Your positioning instinct is the sharpest thing in this doc — write it down and use it.**
   AI agencies sell hours and one-off automations at high-ticket prices; you're productizing
   the whole solution at a flat monthly price. That's a real category difference: they sell
   integration effort, you sell an OUTCOME (answered line, booked jobs, reviews on Google).
   The complexity you're carrying is only justified BECAUSE it's productized — a bespoke shop
   with this scope would be unsellable. Corollary: never pitch the complexity. Pitch the
   phone that answers itself; the platform is what they discover in week two.
4. **Self-serve + waitlist + weekly cohort is the right call — and it makes the "service
   business" identity temporary by design.** You're a service business for the first ~20
   customers (concierge onboarding, hand-held), and the product absorbs the service one
   feature at a time (Sarah-chat onboarding replaces YOU doing setup calls). DOGFOOD.md §5 is
   literally that absorption list. That's the plan: be the service, then ship the service.
5. **One warning: a waitlist with nobody on it is a landing page, not a strategy.** Outreach
   isn't "after everything" — waitlist-FILLING outreach (content, DMs, the demo video) can
   start before onboarding is polished, because the list buys you time and proof of pull.
   Decide a–c today, then send the first ten DMs this week, pointed at the list.
6. **On "post a feature every day": careful.** Your audience is owners, not builders — a
   feature-a-day reads like a changelog and owners don't buy changelogs. Keep the daily
   rhythm, but let the FRAME rotate (the five pillars): the same shipped feature lands 10×
   harder as "here's the 9pm call you're missing" than as "today I pushed the schedule
   module." And protect the streak: batch on Sunday, keep 5 posts banked, never let content
   eat a build day — you're a product company that posts, not a media company that codes.
