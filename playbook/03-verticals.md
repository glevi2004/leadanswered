# Verticals — who we target

## The first 3 clients: whoever pays

You'll hear "niche down" advice everywhere and it's right — but new agency founders niche too narrow too early. For the first three, take whoever signs a contract. Real estate agent, insurance agency, gym, med spa, plumber — doesn't matter.

We need money, case studies, and pattern recognition. We'll learn more from 3 clients in 3 different verticals than from 10 hours of research.

## Around client 4–5: pick the vertical we most enjoyed

Not the most lucrative — **the one where we didn't dread the calls.** That's the one we grind on for the next year.

## Verticals that work for lead follow-up

### Med spas / aesthetics (probably the best fit)
- High-ticket services ($300–$3,000)
- Lots of inbound leads from Instagram / Meta ads
- Owners are savvy enough to buy AI but not technical enough to build it
- Willing to pay $1,500–$2,500/mo

### Real estate teams (5–20 agents — not solo agents)
- Real lead volume, has a designated ops person
- Solo agents are price-sensitive and flaky — skip them

### Insurance agencies (independent P&C agents)
- High lead volume from quote forms
- Very repetitive qualification — easy to automate

### Gym / fitness studios
- High lead volume but lower budgets ($300–$500/mo)
- Volume play, not value play

### Home services with active ad spend
- Plumbers, HVAC running Google LSAs and Meta ads
- Constant lead flow they fail to follow up on
- Cambridge / Boston has hundreds of these

### B2B services
- IT / MSPs, accounting firms, consultancies
- Higher ACV ($1,500–$3,000/mo)
- Longer sales cycles but stickier

## Avoid (for now)

- **Law firms** — regulated, paranoid about liability
- **Healthcare / dental** — HIPAA complications
- **Anything with state licensing** — same reason
